# Operating guide

Version 1.0.0 · Public companion · 1 November 2026.

Use the layers to organize work, not to certify an entity or calculate a universal reputation score. Agree on the entity, audience, market, language and decision context before collecting anything. Record which descriptions are desired separately from the facts that have actually been established.

## 1. Perception Intelligence

**Entry:** an agreed scope and a list of identity variants, peers and audience questions.

1. Record the date, market, language, engine/interface and account conditions of every observation.
2. Capture the full answer or search observation with its sources; distinguish absent answers from failed collection.
3. Review whether the answer concerns the intended entity rather than a namesake.
4. Separate identity errors, missing information, unsupported claims and citation gaps.

**Output:** a baseline log, source inventory and a short diagnosis. **Gate:** another reviewer can reconstruct the conditions and trace each finding to an artifact. An inaccessible answer is not recorded as absence.

## 2. Narrative Positioning

**Entry:** baseline findings plus approved records of the person's or organization's activity.

1. State the audience and the specific decisions the identity should help them make.
2. Draft a factual short description, a longer biography or company profile and a controlled vocabulary.
3. Break measurable assertions into individual claims and assign evidence owners.
4. Mark unsubstantiated, aspirational and confidential claims before publication.

**Output:** a reference narrative and claim register. **Gate:** every factual assertion has a review disposition; an aspiration is not represented as a completed result.

## 3. Entity Foundation

**Entry:** an approved narrative and a set of official identity sources.

1. Establish a canonical entity identifier, name and explicitly approved name variants.
2. Inventory official URLs and relationships, including role dates and parent/subsidiary distinctions.
3. Compare the record with supplied profile observations; review missing data separately from disagreement.
4. Prioritize corrections on channels controlled by the entity, and document requests to third parties.

**Output:** an identity record and correction backlog. **Gate:** no namesakes or historical roles are silently merged. Structured data must describe visible, supported information; it is not a shortcut to recognition.

## 4. Evidence and Source Authority

**Entry:** discrete claims and a source inventory.

1. Determine what each source actually supports and whether it concerns the same entity and time period.
2. Record publisher ownership or affiliation where known; mark uncertain relationships as unknown.
3. Group syndications and repeated publishers so copies are not counted as independent corroboration.
4. Preserve contrary evidence and uncertainty, including when a supporting source has become inaccessible.

**Output:** a claim-to-source map and evidence gaps. **Gate:** a URL alone never counts as verification; human support judgments and their dates are visible.

## 5. AI Search Optimization

**Entry:** a stable identity, useful public proof and clearly scoped discovery problems.

1. Inspect crawl access, response status, indexation directives, canonicals, internal links and visible content.
2. Verify indexing through appropriate search-engine tools rather than inferring it from HTTP 200.
3. Publish useful answers, definitions, original analysis and evidence relevant to audience questions.
4. Link related material coherently; maintain factual consistency across languages and channels.
5. Retest the issue after changes and preserve implementation dates.

**Output:** a technical/editorial backlog and verified implementation notes. **Gate:** no claimed ranking or citation effect is inferred solely from a page change. See [Google's guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide).

## 6. Monitoring and Governance

**Entry:** baseline conditions, an owner for the entity record and an agreed review schedule.

1. Repeat comparable observations and record any change in model, interface, locale or sampling.
2. Triage factual errors separately from normal answer variation.
3. Assign corrections to owners and track requests, evidence, outcomes and unresolved disputes.
4. Review the narrative after role, product, ownership or market changes.

**Output:** a dated review log and an action queue. **Gate:** observations, interpretation and recommendations remain distinct. Attribution of a change to an intervention requires evidence beyond a simple before/after comparison.
