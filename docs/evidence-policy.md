# Evidence review policy

A claim is a specific assertion about an identified entity and time period. A source is a record that may support or contradict it. A link between them is a review judgment, not automatic proof.

## Required record

Give claims stable IDs. Record source URL, publisher, publication date where available, access/review date, relationship to the subject, relevant passage/location, support judgment, reviewer and limitations. Preserve the original wording of the claim being evaluated.

## Review dispositions

- **Supports:** the reviewed material supports the specific claim within its stated limits.
- **Partial:** it supports only part, a different scope or an incomplete time period.
- **Contradicts:** it supplies contrary information that needs resolution.
- **Unreviewed:** relevance/support has not been assessed.

Record publication and independence separately. Owned material can be relevant for a declared position; it does not become independent corroboration. Unknown ownership stays unknown. Repeated URLs, press-release copies and syndication must not be counted as multiple independent publishers.

## Review age

A stale review means someone needs to check it again; it does not automatically make a source false. A missing access date is unknown age, not evidence of freshness. Record broken links without erasing the history, and use lawful archives or retained artifacts only when appropriate.

## Correction

Keep contrary sources visible. Open a review item, identify the source of the disputed fact and assign an owner. Record correction requests and their outcome. A corrected owned biography does not establish that all third-party systems updated their records.

Use [Authority Evidence Mapper](https://github.com/mhdteto/authority-evidence-mapper) for offline organization of these records. It does not verify sources or replace independent review.
