# Can a reader check your expertise claim?

Use this worksheet before publishing a biography, service page or organization profile. Choose one important claim, inspect the supporting material and decide which wording the record supports.

## A worked example

**Fictional teaching material.** Northline Advisory, its publications, events and records below are invented. The review date is 18 November 2026. These examples illustrate editorial decisions and contain no measured search or AI results.

| Proposed claim | Fictional material reviewed | Relationship and support judgment | Wording selected for publication |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| “We have offices in London, Paris and Singapore.” | An owned service page lists those markets. No office records are supplied. | Owned. **Partial** for service coverage, with the office claim unresolved. | “We offer advisory services to organizations in the UK, France and Singapore.” Publish after the service owner confirms current coverage. |
| “Our founder regularly speaks at international conferences.” | One organizer's archived agenda lists a session by the founder at a fictional event, Forum Meridian, in June 2026. | Independent organizer in this scenario. **Partial** for the recurring and international claim. | “Our founder presented a session at Forum Meridian in June 2026.” |
| “Our research is cited by leading AI assistants.” | An owned article links to three research notes. No dated AI answers or visible citations are supplied. | Owned. **Unreviewed** for the AI citation claim because supporting observations are missing. | “We publish research notes on public identity and evidence review.” Link to the notes and describe their contents accurately. |

The service page, agenda and research notes above are invented records. In a real review, open the original sources and record their exact locations before selecting wording. An event appearance supports the specified participation within the agenda's scope. Assess the event's location, audience and recurrence separately when those details matter to a claim.

## Review one claim of your own

1. Copy the exact sentence you plan to publish. Include the person or organization, activity, market and time period it refers to.
2. Open each supporting source. Locate the passage, entry or artifact relevant to that sentence.
3. Record who published it and their relationship to the subject. Mark unknown relationships as unknown. Check whether several pages reproduce the same underlying material.
4. Select a review judgment using the repository's [evidence policy](../docs/evidence-policy.md). Keep contrary information in the record and assign someone to resolve it.
5. Write the approved sentence, the remaining conditions and the next review trigger. A changed role, withdrawn publication or ended service can trigger another review.

## Use the result

Place the approved description on the page that explains your work and link to relevant public evidence. Carry dates and scope into language adaptations. Record each published version so future changes can be traced.
