# International adaptation

International scope requires source and language decisions, not a country name appended to a generic page.

## Define the market context

Record the jurisdiction or market, target audience, languages and scripts, decision context and source categories that matter. Distinguish the place the entity operates from the location of an evaluator or user. Avoid claiming an office, team, client or local credential without supporting records.

## Identity across languages

Maintain canonical legal/professional names and explicitly approved transliterations. Preserve diacritics in the public record. Record former names and role dates. A translated title may need human approval rather than exact-string equivalence. Do not merge similarly named entities across countries.

## Source selection

Choose sources based on their relevance to the claim: official filings for legal identity, original publications for authorship, appropriate professional registers for regulated credentials, and independent reporting for third-party descriptions. Availability, authority and independence are separate questions. Record who published the material and when it was accessed.

## Compare cautiously

Run separate prompt sets by market/language and explain any translation or intent differences. Keep source accessibility, interface location and account conditions in the log. Differences between two countries' sampled answers are not proof of nationality bias or a stable ranking.

## Operating checklist

- [ ] Audience and market are explicit.
- [ ] Identity variants are reviewed by someone competent in the language.
- [ ] Source categories reflect the claims and jurisdiction.
- [ ] Unavailable information is marked unknown.
- [ ] Legal questions go to qualified counsel; this toolkit is not legal advice.
- [ ] Results retain engine, language, market and date labels.
